1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & DATASET PROVENANCE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This comprehensive Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) investigates student academic performance
in secondary education across Mathematics (N=395) and Portuguese Language (N=649) cohorts.
The analysis was executed strictly on clean working datasets in data/processed/,
leaving raw source files in data/raw/ completely untouched and preserved.

2. DATASET CHARACTERISTICS & AGGREGATE PERFORMANCE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Mathematics Cohort:  395 students, 33 variables. Mean G3: 10.42 (Std: 4.58), Median: 11.0
  Pass Rate (G3 >= 10): 67.1%, Zero-Grade Dropout Rate (G3=0): 9.6%
- Portuguese Cohort:   649 students, 33 variables. Mean G3: 11.91 (Std: 3.23), Median: 12.0
  Pass Rate (G3 >= 10): 84.6%, Zero-Grade Dropout Rate (G3=0): 2.3%
- Key Observation: Portuguese students demonstrate substantially higher overall achievement and pass rates
  with tighter dispersion, whereas Mathematics exhibits higher variance and ~4.2x higher rate of zero grades.

C. Strongest Positive and Negative Relationships:
   1. Prior Academic Attainment (G2 and G1):
      - G2 is the single strongest predictor of G3 (Math r = 0.905, Por r = 0.919).
      - G1 is the second strongest predictor (Math r = 0.801, Por r = 0.826).
      - Academic momentum is highly consistent across trimesters.
   2. Historical Failure Burden (failures):
      - Failures is the strongest non-grade negative factor (Math r = -0.360, Por r = -0.393).
      - Each historical failure lowers expected final grade by ~3 to 5 grade points.
   3. Study Effort (studytime):
      - Positively associated with achievement, particularly in Portuguese (r = +0.250, rho = +0.250).
      - In Math, study time shows weaker linear correlation (r = +0.098), though mean grades improve up to 5-10 hrs/wk.
   4. Socioeconomic & Parental Capital (Medu, Fedu):
      - Mother's education (Math r = +0.217, Por r = +0.240) and Father's education (Math r = +0.152, Por r = +0.212)
        exert persistent, statistically significant positive influences on achievement.
   5. Alcohol Consumption & Social Outings (Dalc, Walc, goout):
      - Negative association with grades, especially in Portuguese (Dalc r = -0.205, Walc r = -0.177).
   6. Absences Non-Linearity:
      - Absence linear correlation is near zero in Math (r = +0.034) and slight negative in Portuguese (r = -0.091).
      - This is driven by structural zeros (G3=0 dropouts had 0 recorded absences) and high-ability students
        maintaining strong grades despite high absenteeism.

5. KEY PATTERNS & COMPARATIVE DIFFERENCES (MATHEMATICS VS. PORTUGUESE)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Gender-Subject Interaction Effect:
   - Mathematics: Male students outperform females (Mean 10.91 vs. 9.97, Diff = +0.95, p = 0.0396).
   - Portuguese:  Female students outperform males (Mean 12.25 vs. 11.41, Diff = +0.85, p = 0.0011).
   - Reflects classical subject-specific gender divergence requiring targeted pedagogical interventions.

2. Aspirations for Higher Education (higher):
   - The single most dramatic categorical predictor across both subjects.
   - Math: Yes (10.61) vs. No (6.80) -> +3.81 point premium (Cohen's d = 0.86, p = 0.0024).
   - Por:  Yes (12.28) vs. No (8.80) -> +3.48 point premium (Cohen's d = 1.15, p = 2.32e-14).

3. School Educational Support (schoolsup) & The Confounding Paradox:
   - Students receiving extra school support achieve LOWER average final grades in both subjects:
     Math: Support (9.43) vs. No Support (10.56), Diff = -1.13, p = 0.0197.
     Por:  Support (11.28) vs. No Support (11.98), Diff = -0.70, p = 0.0268.
   - Interpretation: Confounding by indication. Remedial support is assigned to struggling learners.
     This serves as a prime real-world demonstration that CORRELATION DOES NOT EQUAL CAUSATION.

4. Geographic & Digital Capital (Urban vs. Rural & Internet Access):
   - Urban students outscore rural peers by +1.16 points in Math and +1.18 points in Portuguese.
   - Students with home internet access outscore those without by +1.21 points in Math and +1.15 points in Portuguese.

6. METHODOLOGICAL LIMITATIONS OF THE ANALYSIS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Observational Design: All relationships are correlational; no randomized controls exist.
2. Self-Reported Metrics: Survey variables (alcohol, free time, study hours) may contain social-desirability bias.
3. Two-School Geographic Clustering: Data stems from two secondary schools in the Alentejo region of Portugal,
   which may not fully generalize to disparate national educational systems without calibration.
4. Unrecorded Qualitative Factors: Teacher quality, classroom dynamic, and psychological stressors are unobserved.

7. SCIENTIFIC PRINCIPLE: CORRELATION DOES NOT IMPLY CAUSATION
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
It is an essential scientific principle of this EDA that observed correlations, statistical dependencies,
and group disparities DO NOT imply direct causation. For example, while school educational support (schoolsup)
exhibits a statistically significant negative correlation with final marks, remedial tutoring does not cause
poor marks; rather, prior academic distress causes selection into remedial assistance.
Similarly, parental education and home internet access correlate with student success largely due to broader
socioeconomic security, learning resources, and household stability rather than solitary mechanisms.
All insights herein should guide holistic intervention strategies rather than naive causal assumptions.
